Method

How models are built

The models are rebuilt by AI coding agents. The agents transcribe each paper's equations, parameter values and dosing into code, digitise the target figures, run the simulations and comparisons, and draft the reports. The author reviews every equation, parameter value, fix and grade against the paper before it is committed. The second implementations on the validation page were written by separate agent sessions with no access to the atlas code. The next phase, a pre-registered study of a random sample of CPT:PSP models, adds blind rebuilds by outside pharmacometricians, to compare with the agent rebuilds.

What counts as reproducing a model

A paper's model is reproduced when the equations and parameter values it publishes, simulated under the protocol it describes, give the curves it shows. The atlas checks this against the authors' own simulation lines, not against observed data: a model can fit its data badly and still be perfectly reproducible, and the reverse.

Each model is graded:

grademeaning
Athe model as published runs and matches every target curve
Bit matches only after documented fixes (typos, unstated choices, text vs code)
Cit runs but does not match, with or without fixes
Dit cannot be run from the published information

B† marks a B that needed an input the paper never states (an initial condition, or which of several published scenarios a figure shows) and that could only be read from the figure being reproduced. That makes the match circular for that one value, so the dagger flags it; the model's equations and parameters are still tested over the whole time course. Parameters read off the target figure are never allowed: that model is D. Every fix must cite its evidence, or it cannot earn a B.

‡ marks a B (or B†) that rests on at least one fix made after the first comparison with the target figure (stage = :after_comparison). Such a fix was chosen with the mismatch in view, so it carries more risk of fitting the figure than a fix decided before any comparison, even when its evidence is independent of the figure. Each fix's stage is listed on the model page.

What may fill a gap. A value the paper leaves out may be supplied only from the paper itself (another section, table or figure that is not a scored target), its supplement, a reference the paper cites for that value, or a standard physical or physiological constant (a molecular weight, IgG mass of about 150 kDa, human blood volume of about 5 L). Each such fill is a documented :missing fix. A model is D only when an essential equation or value has none of these sources. Values chosen by matching the scored figure are :from_target (B†) for protocol inputs and never allowed for parameters. This rule was written after reviews had argued for D on three of the models (Fornari, Lindauer, Stroh), and it is what keeps them at C. To show its effect, every model also has a strict grade: under the strict reading only the article and its supplement count as published information, and a model that needs any value from elsewhere (a cited reference, a standard value, an assumption or the target figure) is D. Both grades are listed on each model page and in the sensitivity tables.

Provenance of every choice. Each fix records its basis (paper, supplement, interpretation, cited reference, standard value, assumption, target figure) and its stage: whether it was chosen before or after the simulation was first compared with the target. A published run that needs gap fills lists them and can never be graded A.

Grades describe the paper, not the effort spent. Re-estimating parameters never counts as a fix, and recalibration results are reported separately without changing a grade. Unfinished work is not graded D.

Targets

For each paper, the grade-bearing targets are the simulation lines in chosen figure panels. They are chosen, digitised and given tolerances before the simulation is compared with them, and the choice is recorded in the model's notes and report.

Digitising

Figures are taken from the open-access article (PubMed Central). tools/digitise traces a curve by colour inside a pixel box and maps pixels to data with a two-tick calibration per axis, linear or logarithmic. The settings are saved in each model's reference/digitise.toml, so every point can be regenerated from the source image. Where colour tracing cannot separate curves (black solid and dashed lines that overlap), points are picked from pixel inspection and the method line says so. Every digitisation is checked by overlaying the points on the original image (figures/digitise_check.png) and against an axis tick not used for calibration.

Scoring

Both axes of the figure panel are scaled to [0, 1] (log axes in decades), using the axis limits as drawn. For each digitised point the error is its shortest distance to the simulated curve in that box. Distance to the curve, rather than vertical error, keeps steep sections (dosing spikes, rapid expansion) from being penalised for tiny horizontal digitising error.

A curve passes when the root mean square error is at most 0.015 and the 95th percentile is at most 0.03 of the axis box. The plot panels digitised so far are 85 to 294 px wide or high, so one pixel is 0.3 to 1.2 % of an axis, and the tolerance corresponds to roughly 1.3 to 3.5 px on the vertical axis.

How the tolerance was measured. A digitising error study (tools/digitise/error_study/) drew about 7,650 known curves at the real panel sizes of the atlas figures, saved them as JPEG at the quality of the PMC files (92), traced them with the atlas tools and rules, and scored them against the known curves with the atlas metric. For traces that pass an overlay check, the 99th percentile of the error ranges from 0.0013 to 0.0077 (RMS) and from 0.0029 to 0.0157 (P95) across panel types; the worst type is a small log-scale panel (180 x 149 px). The atlas references were also compared with the same curves taken from the first authors' theses (vector or higher-resolution figures) for 34 curves: worst RMS 0.0080, worst P95 0.0107. The rule "twice the worst panel type's 99th percentile, at two significant figures" gives RMS 0.015 and P95 0.031; the atlas keeps 0.015 and 0.03. The factor of two covers the gap between real and synthetic errors (real errors were 0.9 to 1.4 times the synthetic medians) and error sources neither part measures (publisher resampling, calibration slips). No committed result changes under the measured rule.

About 4 % of synthetic traces failed grossly (the tracer jumped to another curve or mixed two lines). Only a visual overlay of the traced points on the figure catches this, so every reference trace is checked that way before it is frozen. On a log axis spanning many decades the same tolerance allows a larger relative error (on a six-decade axis, RMS 0.015 corresponds to a uniform factor of about 1.23); the sensitivity of every grade to the tolerance is published. The 95th percentile instead of the maximum limits the effect of a single badly traced point, but does not remove it: one point 0.12 of the axis away fails the RMS criterion on its own for a 60-point curve. The maximum is reported. A model may set a different tolerance for a curve, with the reason stated before comparing.

The nearest-distance metric scores only the regions where the figure has digitised points: a simulated feature with no reference points nearby costs nothing. Every curve therefore reports diagnostics alongside its pass or fail (score.toml and the model pages): coverage, the share of the axis x range spanned by the digitised reference; sim_coverage, the share where the simulation can be placed on the axes; a reverse distance (rev_rms, rev_p95) from the simulated curve back to the reference; and for bands, the deviation from the band centre in band half-widths and, where a model supplies its own simulated interval edges, the error of those edges. Some passes rest on little of the axis (Lindauer's base case is two points; Stroh fig4c_mAb covers 11 % of its axis).

These diagnostics are report-only for the first six models: they were added after those models were graded, and turning them into pass criteria now would change grades after the results were known. For models added from now on, a minimum coverage and a reverse-distance criterion will be fixed before any comparison.

Population figures

Figures that show population spread (visual predictive checks) are graded only on the authors' simulated quantities: shaded intervals of simulated percentiles are band references, scored as zero inside the interval and as the vertical distance to the nearer edge outside it; simulated percentile lines use the vertical metric, compared at matched times. Lines computed from observed data are never grade-bearing. The cohort design, bins, quantile definition, residual error and number of replicates are fixed before comparing, and replicates are added until the Monte Carlo error is small next to the tolerance. See Population simulation.

Regression testing

test/runtests.jl runs every models/*/reproduce.jl in a fresh module and checks that the grade and every curve's pass/fail equal the committed score.toml. A change in a solver, ModelingToolkit or the model code that moves a result fails CI.
